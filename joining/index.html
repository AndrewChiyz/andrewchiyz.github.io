🎓 Join Us

We welcome master’s applicants, including recommendation-based admission applicants, and undergraduate students who want to pursue sustained research in computer vision and machine learning.

Our work spans video analysis and synthesis, human motion transfer, object pose estimation, salient object detection, and medical image segmentation. We focus on research questions, reproducible experiments, and the development of independent research skills.

Master’s students

We welcome students applying to academic or professional master’s programmes at the School of Computer Science and Technology, Guangdong University of Technology, including applicants using recommendation-based admission. You may email a CV to discuss your research interests and potential supervision.

  • Have a clear interest in computer vision or machine learning and be willing to explore research questions over an extended period.
  • Be prepared to read papers, implement methods, conduct experiments, and present progress regularly.
  • Aim for substantial research training and high-quality outcomes. The group places demanding expectations on research progress and thesis work.

Undergraduate researchers

The usual applicants are first- to third-year students at the GDUT School of Computer Science, particularly those considering postgraduate or doctoral research in China or abroad.

  • Be willing to learn the prerequisites and make a sustained commitment to a research topic.
  • Once a topic is agreed, take part in regular paper reading, experiments, and weekly presentations.
  • Plan your commitments carefully. Dividing attention across multiple teams should not prevent consistent participation in the group’s work.
  • Before joining, an introductory research task and a one-to-one discussion help both sides understand the working style and expectations.

Preparation & basic requirements

  • Mathematics: linear algebra, probability, and statistics.
  • Subject knowledge: machine learning and deep learning fundamentals; fill any gaps through coursework or self-study.
  • Implementation: be willing to understand research code, reproduce experiments, and explain the implementation and results.
  • Research habits: active learning, clear communication, careful reasoning, and persistence when experiments do not work as expected.

Suggested starting points: Stanford CS231n；Machine Learning by Zhi-Hua Zhou, introductory machine learning courses by Andrew Ng, and linear algebra courses by Gilbert Strang.

Research training & working arrangements

  • Weekly presentations: discuss papers or report progress using slides, covering the problem, method, experiments, and next steps.
  • Research practice: develop paper-reading, coding, experimental analysis, academic writing, and presentation skills.
  • Research goals: work towards high-quality journal and conference papers. Where appropriate, research outcomes can also support invention patent applications and research-oriented student competitions.
  • Sustained effort: good research ideas and papers take time to develop, test, revise, and refine.

Application materials & contact

Please email the following materials and briefly state whether you are interested in master’s supervision or undergraduate research. Recommendation-based admission applicants are welcome to send a CV.

  1. CV: educational background, relevant coursework, and English proficiency results, where applicable.
  2. Research or project experience: describe previous work and your contribution, if any.
  3. Interests and plans: research topics you would like to explore and your plans for further study or research.

Research discussion & introductory tasks

After initial contact, we can arrange a brief conversation online or in person to discuss interests and expectations. An introductory task may then be arranged to help applicants experience the group’s research practice.

1. Paper presentation

Read an assigned or self-selected paper over approximately one week and prepare a 10–15 minute slide presentation.

  • Identify the venue and relevant research background.
  • Explain the core problem, motivation, and main categories of existing approaches.
  • Explain the proposed method, key technical principles, and workflow.
  • Summarize the method and its main contribution concisely in your own words.

2. Experimental reproduction

Over approximately one week, study an assigned or self-selected paper, reproduce its experiments where feasible, and present the implementation logic and results using slides. Follow this with a one-to-one discussion.

These activities are research discussions and introductory group tasks; they are separate from the university’s formal admissions assessments.
